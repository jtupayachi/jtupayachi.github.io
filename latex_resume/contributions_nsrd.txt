OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Under the Nuclear Safety Research and Development (NSRD) program, funded
through the DOE Office of Environmental Management, my work centered on
strengthening the reliability of the meteorological data that feeds
atmospheric dispersion modeling at the Oak Ridge Reservation. Accurate
dispersion predictions are only as trustworthy as the sensor data behind
them, and degraded, drifting, or malfunctioning meteorological stations
can silently corrupt the models that emergency-response and nuclear-safety
decisions depend on. My contributions focused on building artificial
intelligence systems that automatically detect, classify, and flag
anomalous station readings before they propagate into downstream safety
analyses, replacing slow and inconsistent manual review with a scalable,
reproducible, and explainable machine learning pipeline.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
CONTRIBUTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Vision Transformer and Foundation Vision Models for CAPARS.
   I designed and built Vision Transformer (ViT) and foundation vision
   models that form the core of a multi-pathway hybrid architecture
   powering the CAPARS automated quality-assurance system. Rather than
   relying on a single model, the architecture combines complementary
   pathways so that the strengths of each compensate for the weaknesses
   of the others, yielding a more robust and generalizable quality-control
   engine across a heterogeneous network of stations.

2. Wind Speed and Direction Classification Across Station Networks.
   I developed models that classify wind speed and direction across a
   distributed network of meteorological stations. Because wind fields are
   the primary driver of how airborne material disperses, reliable
   classification of these variables is essential for trustworthy
   dispersion modeling. The models learn the expected behavior of each
   station and surface readings that deviate from physically plausible
   patterns, directly supporting the integrity of atmospheric dispersion
   simulations.

3. Explainable Machine Learning for Extreme Weather Prediction.
   I designed explainable machine learning models for extreme weather
   event prediction using large-scale atmospheric, environmental, and
   sensor datasets. Explainability was a deliberate design requirement:
   in a nuclear-safety context, analysts and reviewers must be able to
   understand and defend why a model raised a flag, so the models expose
   the features and signals driving each prediction rather than acting as
   an opaque black box.

4. Automated Data-Quality and Anomaly-Detection Pipelines.
   I implemented automated data-quality and anomaly-detection checks over
   large-scale atmospheric and geospatial datasets. These pipelines
   continuously validate incoming station data, catch sensor drift and
   outliers, and reduce the manual burden of quality review, making the
   overall quality-assurance process faster, more consistent, and more
   auditable.

5. Vision Transformer-Based Quality Assurance Engine.
   I contributed to the development of a Vision Transformer-Based Quality
   Assurance Engine for wind speed and direction classification, which
   consolidates the above work into a unified engine and is being prepared
   as a forthcoming publication.

6. Context-Aware Visual Prompting for Geospatial Decision-Support
   Dashboards.
   I contributed to a context-aware visual prompting system that uses
   large language models to automatically generate and configure
   geospatial web dashboards for decision support. The approach lets
   non-expert users drive dashboard creation and spatial analysis through
   natural language, while an agentic self-validation loop checks and
   corrects the model's own outputs to improve reliability. This work
   turns complex geospatial and atmospheric datasets into interactive,
   decision-ready visualizations that support nuclear-safety and
   environmental-monitoring workflows, and is documented in the
   "Context-Aware Visual Prompting" paper listed below.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
IMPACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Together, these contributions move meteorological quality assurance from
a labor-intensive, manual process toward an automated, explainable, and
scalable AI system. By catching degraded or anomalous station data early,
the work improves the trustworthiness of atmospheric dispersion modeling
and, in turn, the nuclear-safety and emergency-response decisions that
rely on it.
